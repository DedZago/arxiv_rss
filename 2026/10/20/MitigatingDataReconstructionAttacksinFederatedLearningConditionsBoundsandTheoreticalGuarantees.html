Mitigating Data Reconstruction Attacks in Federated Learning: Conditions, Bounds, and Theoretical Guarantees


Xiaojin Zhang, Mingcong Xu, Wei Chen, Qiang Yang
[stat.ML]

Privacy attacks, particularly data reconstruction attacks, pose significant challenges to federated learning despite its promise in privacy-preserving collaborative training. This paper presents a comprehensive theoretical analysis of privacy leakage in federated learning through dual perspectives: linear algebra and optimization theory. By analyzing the client-update Jacobian, we identify conditions under which distinct data batches produce the same model update and derive a sufficient batch-size criterion for non-unique reconstruction. Through optimization theory, we establish a novel upper bound for privacy leakage that incorporates batch size, distortion extent, and other critical parameters. These theoretical insights elucidate the fundamental relationships between system parameters and privacy vulnerability, laying the groundwork for developing more robust privacy-preserving federated learning frameworks.
