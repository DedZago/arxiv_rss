The Certification Exponent: Instance-Optimal Sequential Certification of Policy Improvement from History Dependent Logs


Rui Wang
[stat.ML]

Before a new policy replaces a deployed one, we often want a certificate, computed from logged episodes, that it is better. We ask how many episodes such a certificate needs when the logging policy is known but history-dependent, and the error probability must stay below $\delta$ in every environment consistent with the logs. The answer is an exponent $K^\star$: the smallest KL divergence from the logged law to a law with the same logged action probabilities under which the target does not improve. No sound certifier stops in fewer than $\log(1/\delta)/K^\star$ expected episodes, at any $\delta$, and whenever $0<\infty$ a plug-in Kelly certifier attains this rate to first order as $\delta\to0$. Coverage, computed by one backward induction, decides whether certification is impossible ($K^\star=0$), possible at a finite rate, or possible in a constant number of episodes ($K^\star=\infty$). By convex duality, the optimal certificate bets on the importance-weighted advantage and also places fair bets on the logged actions, so doubly robust control variates act as hedges rather than estimators. As a consequence, no choice of off-policy estimator changes $K^\star$, a coverage ceiling explains the curse of memory, and structural assumptions can be priced in exponent units. A cushion certifier extends the approach to trees too large to enumerate. In experiments the plug-in’s median stopping time is 1.13-1.43x the lower bound on memory tasks, 1.13-1.51x on Tiger ($H\le5$) and 1.17-1.64x on random trees. The best scalar betting baselines need up to 2.65x (memory) and 4.1x (Tiger) the plug-in’s episodes; only on a few shallow random trees is a doubly robust bet faster, by at most 18%.
