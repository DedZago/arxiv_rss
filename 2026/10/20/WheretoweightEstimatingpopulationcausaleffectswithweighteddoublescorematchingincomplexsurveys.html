Where to weight? Estimating population causal effects with weighted double score matching in complex surveys


Yukang Zeng, Guangyu Tong, Jiaqi Tong, Haidong Lu, Bhramar Mukherjee, Fan Li
[stat.ME]

Double score matching is an emerging method for causal inference that combines propensity and prognostic scores to achieve dimension reduction and model robustness. However, when the observational data are drawn from a complex survey, the existing literature does not provide sufficient guidance on the proper use of survey weights in matching strategies for population-level causal inference. We develop weighted double score matching (WDSM) that provides a unified framework for incorporating survey weights throughout the score estimation, matching, and outcome analysis under both retrospective (informative) and prospective (non-informative) sampling designs. We establish asymptotic normality of the resultant effect estimates under both known and estimated scores and develop a linearization-based bootstrap procedure for variance estimation that preserves the survey-weighted matching structure. Simulation studies show that WDSM substantially reduces bias relative to unweighted matching when either the propensity score model or the prognostic score models are correctly specified. Under correct specification, WDSM also improves efficiency and confidence interval coverage relative to survey-weighted propensity score matching, with particularly pronounced gains under poor overlap. Applications to early marijuana use and mental health in the National Survey on Drug Use and Health and to sleep duration and glycemic control in the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey illustrate the practical value of WDSM for population-level inference. We conclude with a discussion of our findings and provide practical guidance on incorporating survey weights into matching-based analyses of observational data.
