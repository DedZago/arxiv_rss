Composite Variable Strategies for Continuous Endpoints


James Bell, Oliver N. Keene, Christian Bressen Pipper, Thomas Drury, Lorenzo Guizzaro, Marian Mitroiu, Tobias Mütze, Khadija Rerhou Rantell, Marcel Wolbers, David Wright
[stat.AP]

Composite endpoints are common in clinical trials for binary, time-to-event or recurrent outcomes. Recently, there has been interest in estimands for continuous endpoints that employ a composite variable strategy for handling intercurrent events, whereby a value on the continuous scale is defined as the outcome. In this paper we describe these continuous composite endpoints where participants who experience the intercurrent event are assigned a pre-specified value on the outcome scale (which we call the failure value) instead of a measurement. We describe these endpoints and the difficulty of choosing the failure value when the mean is the chosen summary statistic. The large impact of the choice of failure value on the variance of the distribution of the endpoint is quantified as a function of the probability of the event and the standardised difference of the failure value from the mean of the unaffected values. Strategies for choosing failure values are discussed, emphasising the need to balance the clinical relevance and interpretability of the estimand with the need to reliably estimate it. Two potential solutions to situations where there is no relevant and viable choice of failure value are proposed; using failure values based on distributional characteristics to generate clinical relevance and using alternative summary measures to avoid the need to select specific values.
