Dual-Phase Flow Matching for Probabilistic Source Detection and Measurement in Astronomical Images


Yicun Duan, Jeffrey Regier
[stat.AP]

Using the largest digital camera ever built, the NSF-DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory’s 10-year Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) will image over half the night sky. Most astronomical analyses of LSST data operate not on images but on catalogs derived from them: tables of light sources detailing their positions, fluxes (colors and brightnesses), and types (e.g., star, galaxy). At LSST’s imaging depths, light sources often overlap visually, so the configuration of sources in each image is itself uncertain. Nevertheless, LSST’s cataloging pipeline commits to a single source configuration, which can bias downstream analyses. Here, we instead approximate a posterior distribution over source configurations. Our posterior approximation is a continuous-time Markov process (CTMP) fitted by generator matching, an extension of flow matching to non-Euclidean state spaces. This CTMP generates a source configuration in two phases: it detects light sources in the first and measures them in the second. On simulated LSST-like data, the proposed method produces better posterior approximations than five comparison methods: two Markov chain Monte Carlo methods that run thousands of times more slowly at survey scales; two neural posterior estimation methods; and one ablation of the proposed method that lacks the dual-phase structure. Applied to LSST Data Preview 1 (DP1), the first public release of Rubin imaging, the proposed method alone recovers small-scale galaxy clustering in agreement with higher-resolution Hubble imaging and reports uncertainty that remains calibrated against that reference as blending increases. We deliver a probabilistic catalog of the Extended Chandra Deep Field South region, one of the most extensively studied extragalactic fields, and use it to characterize the galaxy color-magnitude distribution.
