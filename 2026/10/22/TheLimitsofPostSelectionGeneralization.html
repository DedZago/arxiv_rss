The Limits of Post-Selection Generalization


Kobbi Nissim, Adam Smith, Thomas Steinke, Uri Stemmer, Jonathan Ullman
[stat.ML]

While statistics and machine learning offer numerous methods for ensuring generalization, these methods often fail in the presence of adaptivity—the common practice in which the choice of analysis depends on previous interactions with the same dataset. A recent line of work has introduced powerful, general purpose algorithms that ensure post hoc generalization (also called robust or post-selection generalization), which says that, given the output of the algorithm, it is hard to find any statistic for which the data differs significantly from the population it came from. In this work we show several limitations on the power of algorithms satisfying post hoc generalization. First, we show a tight lower bound on the error of any algorithm that satisfies post hoc generalization and answers adaptively chosen statistical queries, showing a strong barrier to progress in post selection data analysis. Second, we show that post hoc generalization is not closed under composition, despite many examples of such algorithms exhibiting strong composition properties.
