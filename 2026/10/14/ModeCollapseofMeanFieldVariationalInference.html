Mode Collapse of Mean-Field Variational Inference


Shunan Sheng, Bohan Wu, Alberto González-Sanz
[stat.ML]

Mean-field variational inference (MFVI) is a widely used method for approximating high-dimensional probability distributions by product measures. It has been empirically observed that MFVI optimizers often suffer from \emph{mode collapse}. For example, when the target measure $\pi$ is a mixture $\pi = w P_0 + (1 - w) P_1$, the MFVI optimizer tends to place most of its mass near a single component of the mixture. This work provides the first \emph{theoretical} explanation of mode collapse in MFVI. Starting from two-component mixtures, we introduce the notion of \emph{$\varepsilon$-separateness} to quantify the separation of the mixture components. We derive explicit bounds on the mass that any MFVI optimizer assigns to each component, when $P_0$ and $P_1$ are $\varepsilon$-separated for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$. We further extend this analysis to mixture target distributions with multiple components. Our results reveal that the occurrence of mode collapse crucially depends on the relative position of the components. To address this issue, we propose \emph{rotational variational inference (RoVI)}, which augments MFVI with a rotation matrix. The numerical studies support our theoretical findings and illustrate improved mode coverage with RoVI in Bayesian mixture posteriors, including a real-data example.
