Nonparametric Kernel Clustering with Bandit Feedback


Victor Thuot , Sebastian Vogt , Debarghya Ghoshdastidar , Nicolas Verzelen
[stat.ML]

Clustering with bandit feedback refers to the problem of partitioning the arms of an $N$-armed stochastic bandit according to their underlying distributions, based on noisy observations collected sequentially and adaptively. This setting has attracted growing interest for its applicability in recommendation systems, crowdsourcing, and adaptive trials. Existing works rely on the strong assumption that arm distributions are sub-Gaussian, which can only distinguish arms whose distributions differ in mean - missing realistic settings where distributional differences lie in shape or higher moments. % We introduce a framework of \emph{nonparametric clustering with bandit feedback}, free of any parametric assumptions regarding the arm distributions. We adopt a kernel-based approach and reformulate the problem as clustering arms according to their kernel mean embeddings in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS). Building on this formulation, we introduce the \kabc\ algorithm, prove that it is $\delta$-PAC, and derive a non-asymptotic high-probability upper bound on its sampling complexity. The bound is governed by a variance-aware signal-to-noise ratio defined as the minimum over pairs of arms in distinct clusters of the ratio between their squared MMD and their RKHS variance. \kabc\ achieves instance-dependent guarantees while being adaptive to this unknown ratio. We validate our approach empirically on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating that \kabc\ succeeds in distributional clustering settings where mean-based methods fail.
