Reference-State Bootstrap Correction and Kernel Characterization of Residual-Based Fit Indices in Structural Equation Models


Eunjeong Song, Sehee Hong
[stat.ME,stat.TH]

Sampling error affects the finite-sample values of residual-based fit indices. We show that the unweighted comparative fit index ($CFI_u$) rests on a quadratic-kernel maximum mean discrepancy and that a Gaussian-kernel comparative fit index (KCFI) converges to $CFI_u$ as bandwidth increases. Because kernel discrepancies lack an analytic sampling correction, we extend expected-discrepancy bootstrap methods to a fitted-correlation reference state: the sample is transformed to reproduce the fitted correlation matrix, then resampled and refitted to estimate the exact-fit sampling component. Across 18 confirmatory factor analysis conditions, this correction reduced the root mean square error (RMSE) of both indices by 62%-87%. Under normality it matched an analytic residual-covariance estimator of $CFI_u$ within .0001, and in small samples it outperformed ordinary bootstrap correction in accuracy and availability. Corrected KCFI had lower RMSE than identically corrected $CFI_u$ in all 29 nonnormal conditions, even against a common target, but in six heavy-tailed small-sample conditions the analytic estimator was more accurate than both. The results validate the reference-state correction where it is needed and delimit where the analytic estimator is preferable.
