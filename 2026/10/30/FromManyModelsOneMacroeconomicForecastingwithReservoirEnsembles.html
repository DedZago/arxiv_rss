From Many Models, One: Macroeconomic Forecasting with Reservoir Ensembles


Giovanni Ballarin, Lyudmila Grigoryeva, Yui Ching Li
[stat.ML]

Model combination offers an alternative to selecting a single forecasting model, particularly when individual models are sensitive to random initialization and hyperparameter choices. We study online aggregation through Follow-the-Leader (FTL) and Hedge-type algorithms, with an application to ensembles of Multi-Frequency Echo State Networks (MFESNs) for macroeconomic forecasting. On the theoretical side, we derive Hoeffding- and Bernstein-type expected-regret bounds for FTL and decreasing Hedge under independent and $\varphi$-mixing expert losses. Under a positive gap between the best expert’s expected loss and those of its competitors, the bounds are uniform in the number of forecasting rounds and exhibit logarithmic dependence on the number of experts. Monte Carlo experiments investigate the effects of temporal dependence and the separation between experts’ expected losses. Empirically, we construct MFESN ensembles for forecasting U.S. quarterly GDP growth using mixed-frequency macroeconomic and financial indicators. The results show that adaptive aggregation, particularly FTL and AdaHedge, achieves substantial reductions in out-of-sample mean squared forecasting error relative to individual MFESN models and conventional forecast combinations.
