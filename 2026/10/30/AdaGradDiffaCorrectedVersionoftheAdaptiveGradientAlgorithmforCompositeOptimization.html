AdaGrad-Diff: a Corrected Version of the Adaptive Gradient Algorithm for Composite Optimization


Matia Bojovic, Saverio Salzo, Massimiliano Pontil
[stat.ML]

Vanilla gradient methods are often highly sensitive to the choice of stepsize, which typically requires manual tuning. Adaptive methods alleviate this issue, with AdaGrad being a prominent example. In this work, we first show that, in composite convex optimization, AdaGrad may fail to exploit the H"older-smoothness of the differentiable term, as its stepsizes may decrease too rapidly, leading to convergence rates arbitrarily close to the nonsmooth $\mathcal O(n^{-1/2})$ rate. Motivated by this limitation, we study AdaGrad-Diff, a block-wise extension of the UniSgd method considered by \cite{rodomanov2024universality}, which accumulates successive gradient differences rather than gradients themselves. In the deterministic Lipschitz-smooth setting, we prove summability of squared gradient differences without assuming bounded iterates, yielding weak convergence to a minimizer in Hilbert spaces. Moreover, we show that, under $\nu$-H"older-smoothness, stepsizes decay no faster than $n^{-(1-\nu)/2}$, thereby avoiding AdaGrad’s failure mechanism and recovering the standard $\mathcal O(n^{-(1+\nu)/2})$ rate. The same approach recovers known stochastic convergence rates under a unified condition covering bounded variance and interpolation. Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed method is more robust than AdaGrad in several practically relevant settings.
