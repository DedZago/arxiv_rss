A Bayesian Copula Dirichlet Process GLM for Longitudinal Data


Shijie Yuan, Entejar Alam, Asmit Chakraborty, Paul Rathouz, Jonathan Schildcrout, Peter Müller
[stat.ME,stat.AP]

We introduce a semiparametric Bayesian generalized linear model (GLM) for longitudinal continuous outcomes that preserves the usual interpretation of the GLM regression structure while leaving the baseline distribution nonparametric. The proposed copula Dirichlet process generalized linear model (CDPGLM) combines a Dirichlet process prior for the baseline distribution, a parametric GLM regression structure for covariate effects, and a copula for longitudinal within–subject dependence. This construction separates modeling the marginal distribution of an individual repeated measurement from modeling the association: the Dirichlet process GLM component defines the marginal density and regression mean, whereas the copula captures dependence among repeated measurements. Compared with a parametric beta mixed–effects model, the CDPGLM provides similarly efficient estimation of regression effects while being substantially more robust to estimate marginal distributions and lower-tail probabilities (the complement of exceedance probability). In an application to data from the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) Lung Health Study, the CDPGLM and a parametric mixed beta model gave comparable inference on regression coefficients, but the CDPGLM more closely matched the empirical marginal distribution and reported more realistic and detailed lower–tail risk summaries.
