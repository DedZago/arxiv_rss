Elimination Geometry II: Integrability, Representation, and Structural Limits


Mian Huang, Xueqin Wang
[stat.ME]

Elimination geometry studies the exact nonnegative defect left after an auxiliary variable is optimized out. Part~II audits this certified-elimination interface through three theorem families. For integrability, local gaps form a repeated partial-elimination tower precisely under nonnegativity, touching, and zero elementary-square circulation. Hodge projection constructs nearest finite-space repairs and separates curvature, certification, and global period taxes. For representation and price, a descent gate characterizes surjective refinements and rules out strictly convex separable, duplication-invariant weight penalties. Dummy and target-calling lifts show that unrestricted lift design is vacuous, whereas the Fidelity-coherence frontier isolates irreducible cost. A modal Gram block classifies unconstrained quadratic lift-coupling germs and yields aligned Gaussian risk ledgers. Bernstein spectral functions preserve graph-Laplacian structure. Graph-universal Markov resolvents exist exactly up to first-order fractional qualification, and universal polynomial spectral penalties reduce to nonnegative Laplacian multiples. This linear limit does not preclude Part~I’s nonlinear CDF repair and sharp CRPS theory. For converses, finite-order target-jet preservation uniquely requires subtraction of the corresponding defect jet, up to flat terms; approximate jets yield quasi-descent and stationary limits. Fixed-marginal coupling obeys a dependence-benefit frontier. Reversing certified KL in entropy-regularized control eliminates dimension-free guarantees, while a fixed measurement preserves all Umegaki defects of a faithful Gibbs family exactly when that family commutes.
