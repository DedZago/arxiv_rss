A Bayesian hierarchical push-forward method for cluster Randomized Controlled Trial power calculations in complex humanitarian settings


Margaret Elliott, Sonia Jain, Adam Richards, Brendan Fries, Ravi Goyal
[stat.ME]

The design and evaluation of public health interventions in humanitarian crises demand rigorous yet flexible methodological approaches. Cluster-randomized controlled trials (cRCTs) are increasingly used to generate evidence in such settings, but trial conduct is frequently complicated by displacement, resource constraints, and cluster dropout. Although Bayesian methods provide a natural framework for hierarchical modeling, probabilistic uncertainty quantification, and the integration of prior knowledge, their use in cRCT design remains limited, particularly with respect to anticipating missingness. This paper introduces a Bayesian design strategy tailored for cRCTs in complex settings, motivated by a forthcoming trial in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, that will assess a community health worker adherence intervention for cardiovascular disease and hepatitis C. Our approach incorporates prior information on cluster and individual-level dropout into power and sample size calculations via a hierarchical model and push-forward distribution framework. Through simulation studies, we evaluate the performance of the proposed method under varying levels of missingness and correlation structures. The approach is then applied to inform the design of the Bangladesh trial. We disseminate our methods via a publicly-available R Shiny app to promote accessibility and usability to investigators. By formalizing Bayesian strategies that anticipate data loss, this work provides a foundation for more robust and efficient cRCTs in humanitarian contexts; therefore, strengthening the evidence base for health interventions in resource-limited and crisis-affected populations.
