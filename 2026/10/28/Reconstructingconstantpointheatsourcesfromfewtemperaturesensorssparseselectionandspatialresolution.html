Reconstructing constant point heat sources from few temperature sensors: sparse selection and spatial resolution


Xiaomei Yang, Zhiliang Deng
[stat.ME]

We investigate the recovery of an unknown number of stationary point heat sources, together with their locations and constant positive strengths, from temperature histories recorded by a few sensors. The reconstruction combines candidate selection by orthogonal matching pursuit or least angle regression with continuous constrained fitting. A noise-based discrepancy rule selects the smallest accepted source count among those tested. The analysis distinguishes source-count selection, individual-parameter recovery, and aggregate estimation. For a close pair, the local single-source residual is quadratic in separation when the projected curvature is nonzero, whereas a compensating unit parameter direction produces a linearized response bounded by a constant times the cube of separation. This difference explains why a pair may be distinguishable from a single source while its individual parameters remain poorly determined. A conditional stability bound controls errors in total strength and the strength-weighted center, and a local Gaussian approximation relates single-source rejection to noise and separation. Numerical experiments assess sensor configuration, temporal sampling, noise, separation, and initialization, with additional tests using independently generated finite-plate data and a three-dimensional model. Correct source-count selection need not imply accurate individual parameters, whereas unresolved pairs can retain accurate aggregate estimates.
