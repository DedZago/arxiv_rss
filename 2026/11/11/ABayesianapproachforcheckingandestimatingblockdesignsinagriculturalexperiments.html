A Bayesian approach for checking and estimating block designs in agricultural experiments


Josefina Lacasa, Ignacio A. Ciampitti, Trevor J. Hefley
[stat.ME,stat.AP]

Spatial blocking is a common technique used in designed agricultural experiments. Spatial blocking, however, requires important assumptions about the spatial dependence patterns in the field. Statistical models of blocked agricultural experiments often show spatial structure in the residuals, suggesting that the original model may benefit from additional assumptions regarding the spatial dependence patterns present in the field. We propose a Bayesian model for checking block membership and estimating spatial patterns missing in the original block design. Our model is particularly motivated by cases with mis-specified blocks. To accomplish this, we embed a regression tree within a Bayesian statistical model to estimate spatial effects with a block-like spatial structure. We illustrate the benefits of our model using simulated data mimicking four typical scenarios we have encountered in our applied experience in agricultural research and using two datasets generated from designed agricultural experiments. We found that our model may serve two purposes: validating the originally designed block layout, or mis-specified block-like spatial structures. Thus, our model provides researchers with a novel tool for analyzing data from designed agricultural experiments.
