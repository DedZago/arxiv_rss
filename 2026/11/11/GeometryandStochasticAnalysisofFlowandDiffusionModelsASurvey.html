Geometry and Stochastic Analysis of Flow and Diffusion Models: A Survey


Ehsan Mirafzali, Razvan Marinescu
[stat.ML]

Flow-based and diffusion-based models have become the principal means of learning and sampling high-dimensional probability distributions, from images and video to proteins, molecules and physical fields, and increasingly language. Each carries a simple law to the law of the data along a curve of probability measures, and behind the variety of their names lie three geometries, those of the state space on which the data live, of the space of probability measures through which the curve passes, and of the space of paths traced by the generating process. A single analytic idea runs through the subject. Every score identity used to train a diffusion model is an integration by parts, on the noise, on the data or on Wiener space, and Malliavin calculus extends this to nonlinear and infinite-dimensional diffusions. We develop these foundations with full definitions and follow them through normalising flows, flow matching and few-step maps, diffusion models and their convergence theory, conditioning, Schr"odinger bridges and stochastic control, and their extension to manifolds, Lie groups, discrete and function spaces and jump processes. We close with statistical physics, renormalisation and, more speculatively, holography, in which the radial direction of anti-de Sitter space can play the role of flow time. A table of the main methods and a collection of open problems complete the survey.
