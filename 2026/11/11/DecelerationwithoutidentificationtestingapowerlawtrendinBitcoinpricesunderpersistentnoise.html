Deceleration without identification: testing a power-law trend in Bitcoin prices under persistent noise


Carlos Baquero, Raquel Menezes
[stat.AP,stat.ME]

Plotted against time since its first block on logarithmic axes, the price of Bitcoin lies close to a straight line, and a power law in time has been proposed as its long-run trend. A good fit is weak evidence: when deviations from a trend are as persistent as a random walk, regressions on time find trends and high $R^2$ where none exist. We first bound what fifteen years of weekly prices can reveal, then test only what is answerable. Using a few low-frequency averages, invariant to the noise scale and calibrated over a declared family of persistent deviations with a four-year cycle and declining volatility, we test constant drift in expected log price against an added log-age term. The procedure was fixed, validated and publicly hashed before it was applied once. Constant drift is rejected under this family at nominal 5%, and a log-age coefficient of 5.7, close to the published exponent, is not rejected. The rejection does not survive allowing persistence beyond a random walk, and a Gompertz curve with a ceiling fits about as well as the power law. Whether deviations revert to the trend cannot be decided. Registered forecasts for 2027-2030 will be scored by future prices.
