When Pass@$k$ Crossovers Are Real - and Why: Paired Inference and the Markov Kernel of RLVR


Chen Yang
[stat.ML]

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) typically improves pass@1 but can fall behind its base model at large sampling budgets $k$, commonly interpreted as sharpening existing solutions. Because adjacent pass@$k$ estimates reuse generations, pointwise error bars cannot establish a crossover. We formulate paired inference on the RL-minus-base difference curve: a prompt-level multiplier bootstrap yields a one-sided dominance test and simultaneous confidence bands requiring both an early gain and a later loss. Among nine public RLVR checkpoints at 3k-16k token limits, only one narrowly meets this criterion before multiplicity adjustment; asymmetric truncation can create, mask, or reverse crossovers. On 1,060 deduplicated competition problems at 32k tokens, two 1.5B models exhibit certified crossovers around $k\approx10$-$60$, while two 7B models show no supported loss. A paired variance decomposition shows why adding prompts improves power more than deeper sampling. Prompts with identical base success probability diverge after RLVR, with dispersion replicating across independent generation halves and violating Lipschitz slope bounds for reasonable deterministic maps. Modeling RLVR as a Markov kernel, Jensen’s inequality shows that conditional dispersion depresses pass@$k$ for $k\ge2$. Once dispersion is modeled, the conditional-mean log-odds slope is below one in every pair: RLVR improves hard prompts on average, while late-$k$ loss comes from a 2%-3% minority of base-solved prompts collapsing onto a repeated modal wrong answer and resisting prefix rescue up to 4,096 tokens. In controlled runs from one base model on 3,000 new problems, all four GRPO arms fall behind near $k\approx200$ and remain behind through $k=4096$, while SFT stays significantly ahead throughout.
