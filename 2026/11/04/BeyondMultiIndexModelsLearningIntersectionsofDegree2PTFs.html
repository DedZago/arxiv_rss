Beyond Multi-Index Models: Learning Intersections of Degree-2 PTFs


Ilias Diakonikolas, Daniel M. Kane, Mingchen Ma, Lisheng Ren, Trung Tran
[stat.ML,stat.TH]

We give an algorithm for PAC learning intersections of $k$ degree-2 polynomial threshold functions over $R^d$ under a Gaussian distribution. Our algorithm achieves classification error $\epsilon$ using $d^{O(k)}2^{O_k(\log^3(1/\epsilon))}$ time and samples, improving over the $d^{O(k^2/\epsilon^2)}$ running time of the polynomial regression approach. To our knowledge, our result is the first to obtain a dimension exponent independent of the accuracy parameter, even for $k=2$. We complement our algorithmic result with an SQ lower bound showing that the complexity dependence of $d^{\Omega(k)}$ is unavoidable. Lifting the input to polynomial features turns the target into an intersection of $k$ halfspaces. This suggests using the first two labeled moments for subspace recovery. In the lifted space, however, these moments can reveal nothing. Our starting point is a Gaussian comparison theorem showing that, for sufficiently regular defining quadratics, the labeled moments needed for subspace recovery are approximately preserved. To handle arbitrary defining quadratics, we establish a new structural result showing that every direction with significant resampling influence can be detected through degree-$O(k)$ moments. These results yield an initial $d^{O(k)}2^{\mathrm{poly}_k(1/\epsilon)}$-time learner using adaptive restrictions. To improve the exponential dependence on $1/\epsilon$, we develop a two-stage dimension-reduction approach that avoids the costly adaptive partitioning. After identifying a subspace containing all directions with large influence, we use generalized regularity and Gaussian comparison to recover the remaining relevant quadratic features through a new derivative-based moment operator. The recovered features support an intersection close to the target. Combining them with robust hinge-loss minimization gives our quasipolynomial-time learner.
