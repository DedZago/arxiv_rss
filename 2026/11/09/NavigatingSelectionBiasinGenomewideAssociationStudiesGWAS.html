Navigating Selection Bias in Genome-wide Association Studies (GWAS)


Ciarrah-Jane S Barry, Tahereh Gholipourshahraki, Zoltán Kutalik, Bjarni J Vilhjálmsson, Esben Agerbo
[stat.ME]

Selection bias arises when inclusion in a genetic study depends on traits, or causes of traits, that are associated with genotype. It is present in volunteer-based biobanks, case-control designs and studies of disease progression, and genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics from affected samples can propagate this bias into post-GWAS analyses, such as polygenic scores (PGS), Mendelian randomization (MR) and genetic correlation analyses. Despite this broad potential impact, selection bias is sometimes ignored in genetic studies. Many methods exist to detect and correct it, but they rely on different assumptions and require specific data. We organise ten methods applicable to GWAS by the decisions an analyst faces: whether to diagnose or correct, which data are available (individual-level or summary-level), and which form of selection is operating (non-representative participation, case-control ascertainment, index events, sex-differential participation or survival to recruitment). For each method, we describe its principle and assumptions, and we compare methods addressing the same form of selection by their assumptions, data requirements and limitations. A companion GitHub repository implements nine methods in R, prepares input for the external SPREG software, and provides worked examples on simulated data with known true effects for each form of selection, one of which we present in full. An interactive tool supports method selection. We conclude with recommendations for bias-aware study design, sensitivity analysis and reporting.
