Risk-set reconstruction balance: zero-targeted diagnostics for artificial censoring in clone-censor-weight analyses


Yuta Nonomiya
[stat.ME,stat.AP]

In clone-censor-weight (CCW) analyses, inverse probability of censoring weighting corrects selection from artificial censoring at protocol deviation. Balance diagnostics, such as the end-of-grace between-strategy standardised mean difference (SMD) and censoring balance, compare different populations, and their zero targets under a correct model are not formally stated. We show that, under positivity and a correct censoring model, the weighted retained individuals reconstruct the risk set before each censoring interval and the baseline population, and we propose risk-set reconstruction balance (RRB), which measures how far they depart from these two populations. Interval RRB has a zero target for every history function, including post-baseline covariates, for which between-strategy SMDs have none. Cumulative RRB keeps a zero target for baseline covariates after outcome events, whereas the end-of-grace SMD acquires a depletion term. In simulations, interval RRB responded to an omitted term’s effect on censoring whether or not the term was prognostic. Under correct models, the exceedance rate of its studentised maximum over a null reference for chance imbalance was 3.5-6.0% (nominal 5%) when the censoring model pooled nine intervals and mostly 1.0-4.5% under censoring in a single interval. With many early events, cumulative RRB stayed near zero, whereas the end-of-grace SMD reached 0.40; under loss to follow-up that the weights ignored, cumulative RRB departed from zero and interval RRB stayed small. In a case study, interval RRB flagged a dispensing indicator outside the censoring model. Interval RRB checks the censoring model, and cumulative RRB the representation of the baseline population.
