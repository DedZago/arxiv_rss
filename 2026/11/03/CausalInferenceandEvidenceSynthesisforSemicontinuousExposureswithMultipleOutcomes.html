Causal Inference and Evidence Synthesis for Semi-continuous Exposures with Multiple Outcomes


Xiaoya Wang, Richard J. Cook, Yeying Zhu, Tugba Akkaya-Hocagil, R. Colin Carter, Sandra W. Jacobson, Joseph L. Jacobson, Nancy Day, Louise M. Ryan
[stat.ME]

Semi-continuous exposure variables, featuring a point mass at zero and a continuous dose in exposed individuals, arise frequently in epidemiology. In such settings, investigators may wish to distinguish the causal effect of exposure at a fixed dose with no exposure, from the dose-response effect among exposed individuals. We develop a two-stage framework for causal inference with semi-continuous exposures and multiple outcomes. The proposed approach yields outcome-specific estimators for these two causal components, establishes their large sample properties, and provides Wald-type tests for homogeneity across outcomes. We further extend the framework to the multi-cohort setting through hierarchical homogeneity testing and evidence synthesis, enabling principled pooling across cohorts and outcomes. We apply the proposed methods to several cohort studies designed to evaluate the effects of prenatal alcohol exposure on executive function, mathematics achievement, and learning and memory. Simulation studies show that the proposed estimators and testing procedures perform well. The framework provides a principled basis for estimation, comparison, and synthesis of causal effects with multiple outcomes and semi-continuous exposures.
