Bayesian Reconciliation of Overlapping Multi Step Forecasts: A Post Processing Framework for Cross Origin Coherence


Oluwatosin Bamidele Fajana, Ricardo S. Ehlers, Marinho G. Andrade
[stat.ME]

Overlapping multi step forecasts often disagree, a form of vertical instability that we term cross origin incoherence. We propose a Bayesian reconciliation framework that treats overlapping forecasts as noisy, biased measurements of a common latent trajectory. Operating as a model agnostic post processing layer, it requires no access to model internals. Simulations demonstrate substantial incoherence reduction and robust latent trajectory recovery, remaining highly resilient (correlation 0.972) even under severe nonlinear stress tests. An empirical application to S&P 500 volatility evaluates AR(1), ETS, and LSTM models. The framework reduces incoherence by over 97% across all models while delivering fully calibrated probabilistic forecasts (93% to 94% coverage). Notably, it improves point accuracy for ETS and LSTM. Furthermore, the estimated horizon specific parameters act as a diagnostic X-ray, revealing hidden biases in neural networks without requiring access to their architecture
