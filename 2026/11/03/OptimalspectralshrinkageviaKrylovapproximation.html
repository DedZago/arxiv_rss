Optimal spectral shrinkage via Krylov approximation


Tianfeng Lu, Geoffrey J. Goodhill, Hong Hu
[stat.ME,stat.ML,stat.TH]

Spectral shrinkage encompasses a broad family of estimators for high-dimensional linear regression that regularize estimation and prediction by applying spectral filters to the sample covariance matrix. Despite its widespread use, achieving oracle-optimal prediction risk under general covariance structures remains challenging, as the oracle filter requires unknown population quantities and has a closed form only for specific covariance models such as isotropic and spiked models. We propose \textit{risk-targeted Krylov fitting} (RTK), a data-driven spectral estimator that adapts to the unknown covariance structure and attains the oracle risk asymptotically. Like partial least squares, RTK operates in an $m$-dimensional Krylov space, but it targets the oracle risk directly by minimizing the leave-one-out risk, which we compute exactly without refitting the model $n$ times. Under general distributions and covariance structures, RTK attains the optimal prediction risk in the Krylov space up to $O(1/n)$. Furthermore, its risk approaches that of the optimal analytic filter with $m$ of order $\log n$. In simulations over a wide range of covariance structures, RTK and a truncated variant were robust to the choice of $m$ and consistently among the spectral estimators closest to the oracle. In applications to single-cell sequencing data and large-scale neural recordings, the proposed methods achieved higher prediction accuracy than competing estimators on most tasks.
