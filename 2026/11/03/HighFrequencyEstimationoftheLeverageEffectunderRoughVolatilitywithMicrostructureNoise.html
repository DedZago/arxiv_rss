High-Frequency Estimation of the Leverage Effect under Rough Volatility with Microstructure Noise


Zhao Chen, Ming Cheng, Christina Dan Wang, Ziyang Xiong
[stat.ME,stat.TH]

This paper develops feasible high-frequency inference for the leverage effect when the spot variance follows rough Volterra dynamics, leverage loadings depend on endogenous rough states, and observed prices may be contaminated by microstructure noise. We define an integrated leverage parameter through a scaled covariation between log prices and spot variance that accounts for their different local fluctuation scales. The scaled realized covariation admits a stable functional central limit theorem with an explicit pathwise centering correction arising from within-interval variation in the leverage loading. A loading regularity condition renders this correction asymptotically negligible, and we verify the condition for direct Volterra loadings and for smooth functions of closed rough states for every Hurst index below one half. We construct a block-based estimator with an exact finite-window correction for the leading proportional bias, together with a nonnegative single-scale estimator of its asymptotic variance. Pre-averaging extends feasible inference to observations with independent and identically distributed microstructure noise. Simulations under the rough Heston model demonstrate accurate finite-sample calibration with and without noise. An empirical application to fifteen years of one-second data on nine U.S. equities and ETFs finds negative median leverage estimates for every asset at weekly and monthly horizons.
