Geodesics and intrinsic statistics of isolated connections in Hermitian positive-definite matrices


Ronald García Reyes, Pedro Antonio Valdés-Sosa
[stat.ME]

Quantifying change in a single covariance, coherence or spectral connection requires a distance that respects the positive-definiteness constraint and holds the surrounding entries fixed. We characterize the Riemannian geometry induced by the affine-invariant metric when one conjugate off-diagonal pair of a Hermitian positive-definite matrix varies under this constraint. A fixed Schur congruence maps the admissible set to an open complex disk and reduces its metric to a two-dimensional form independent of the ambient matrix dimension. The disk is complete, with Gaussian curvature in $(-1/4,0]$, and has unique minimizing geodesics and globally defined logarithms. Its intrinsic distance strictly exceeds the ambient affine-invariant distance between distinct points. Conservation of angular momentum reduces the geodesic boundary-value problem to a single scalar equation with elementary angular and length integrals, enabling distance and logarithm evaluation without shooting. For a declared reference distribution with a finite second intrinsic moment, we establish a unique Fr'echet centre and consistency of its empirical estimate, together with a sandwich central limit theorem under the stated regularity conditions. Tangent covariance standardization yields moment identities, and exchangeable rank calibration supplies finite-sample score guarantees. These results provide a geometric and statistical framework for isolated connections on a fixed complement, with explicit limits on extension to varying complements.
