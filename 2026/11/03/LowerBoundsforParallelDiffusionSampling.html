Lower Bounds for Parallel Diffusion Sampling


Yiwen Kou, Yimeng Wang
[stat.ML]

Standard diffusion samplers generate samples through sequential evaluations of a learned score function, with each query depending on previous answers. Parallel samplers seek to accelerate this process by making multiple score queries simultaneously. We ask how many adaptive rounds are necessary when each round allows polynomially many queries. We establish the first polynomial lower bounds on the round complexity of diffusion sampling with approximate scores satisfying a mean-square accuracy guarantee. Specifically, we prove (1) a $\widetilde{\Omega}(d^{1/3})$-round lower bound for sampling smooth, near-isotropic Gaussian mixtures in $\mathbb{R}^d$, and (2) a tight $\Omega(d)$-round lower bound for uniform sampling from anisotropic axis-aligned boxes contained in the unit ball. Both bounds hold for arbitrary randomized algorithms making polynomially many queries per round at arbitrary locations and noise levels, with inverse-polynomial score error and constant total variation accuracy. Our near-isotropic construction follows the blockwise approach of earlier lower bounds for parallel matroid search and discrete sampling \cite{KUW85,AGR24}. We use independent coordinate blocks containing random codebooks with well-separated rates and construct approximate score oracles whose responses in each block depend on consistency tests in earlier blocks. Together, the rate separation and these tests ensure that each round reveals information about at most one additional block with high probability.
