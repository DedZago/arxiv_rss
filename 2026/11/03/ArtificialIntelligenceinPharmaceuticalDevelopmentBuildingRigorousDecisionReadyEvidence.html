Artificial Intelligence in Pharmaceutical Development: Building Rigorous, Decision-Ready Evidence


Xin Luo, Margaret Gamalo, Achilleas Livieratos, Maria Kudela, Qilu Yu, Yunxun Wang, Junjing Lin, Yuxi Zhao, Allshine Chen
[stat.AP,stat.OT]

Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) are reshaping the pharmaceutical lifecycle, from discovery and clinical development through manufacturing and regulatory review to safety surveillance and health economics, creating evidentiary challenges that predictive performance alone cannot address. This review examines the inferential, causal, validation, and documentation requirements for AI-enabled evidence generation in high-stakes settings and argues that they must be built into workflows. We outline the evidentiary functions that AI-derived outputs must satisfy before supporting consequential decisions: estimand definition, fit-for-purpose data collection, bias control, uncertainty quantification, validation, interpretability, and decision-grade documentation. Because AI both depends on and accelerates large-scale, non-randomized data collection, the integration of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) with real-world data (RWD) is where its evidentiary risks concentrate; we therefore present a five-step causal roadmap for that integration, in which external data supplement but never replace a concurrent control, and characterize the safeguards required across development, pharmacovigilance, regulation, and health technology assessment. Priority frontiers answer recurring implementation problems: conformal prediction for overconfident predictions, randomization-based inference for scarce data, and federated causal inference and privacy-preserving generative modeling for data that cannot be pooled. Evidence generation depends not on competition between RCTs and RWD, or between statistics and AI/ML, but on their principled synthesis under causal and inferential frameworks, with statistical expertise central to decision-ready evidence.
