Robust normality transformation and outlier detection in diverse distributions, with application to functional neuroimaging data


Fatma Parlak, Saranjeet Singh Saluja, Amanda Mejia
[stat.ME]

Normality transformations improve conformity with parametric assumptions and, for outlier detection, facilitate parametric thresholds. However, transformation parameters must be estimated from data that may contain contamination, which can distort the transformation of non-outlying observations and lead to masking and swamping. Recently developed robust power transformations address skew but not more general departures from normality, and they remain vulnerable to outliers due to reliance on Huber M-estimators of location and scale, which are heavily biased under contamination. Here, we propose a novel robust transformation based on the flexible sinh–arcsinh (SHASH) family of distributions, which accommodates skewness, non-Gaussian tail weights, and combinations thereof. To robustly estimate the transformation, we consider two methods for initializing outliers: a conventional distance-based approach and a novel variant of anomaly detection. Through extensive simulations and real data analyses, we find that robust SHASH transformation more successfully transforms non-outlying observations toward normality and better identifies outliers at contamination levels up to 20%, compared with existing approaches. In an application to fMRI volume censoring using robust MCD distances, which are approximately $F$-distributed under Gaussianity, robust normality transformation improves distinction of artifactual volumes, illustrating its potential as a preprocessing step for automatic, robust multivariate outlier detection in large neuroimaging studies.
