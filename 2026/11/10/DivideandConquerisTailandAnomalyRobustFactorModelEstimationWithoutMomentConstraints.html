Divide-and-Conquer is Tail- and Anomaly-Robust: Factor Model Estimation Without Moment Constraints


Zetai Cen, Han Yan, Haeran Cho
[stat.ME,stat.TH]

We study robust estimation of elliptical factor models with abnormal observations and without requiring the existence of any positive moments. In particular, a divide-and-conquer (DC) estimator averages orthogonal projectors onto the full column spaces of small data blocks, and we rigorously establish its probability bound that allows weak and heterogeneous factors. The derived convergence rate is comparable to that of classical principal component estimators, up to the proportion of anomalies. Building on our DC estimator, we propose a split-sample tail- and anomaly-robust (STAR) estimator for the factor number using sample medians of successive projected coordinate ratios, which is shown consistent under mild conditions. Subsequent factor and common component estimation is also discussed, with the rates of convergence spelled out. Remarkably, given an upper bound on the true factor number, our suite of procedures are free from tuning, require no tail-dependent truncation threshold nor solving an optimization problem, admit parallel block computations, and hence has the same computational complexity as the classical principal component methods. Extensive experiments corroborate our theoretical findings, where DC and STAR are comparable and often outperform state-of-the-art competitors. We also illustrate our methods on a portfolio return dataset.
