No Universal Winner in Model Selection: Regression versus Representational Similarity Analysis


Chuanji Gao, Gang Chen, Svetlana V. Shinkareva, Rutvik H. Desai
[stat.ME,stat.CO]

Selecting among competing computational models is central to cognitive psychology and neuroscience, yet the relative model-selection performance of regression and representational similarity analysis (RSA) remains unclear. We compared these approaches using Monte Carlo simulations and empirical data. Univariate simulations compared generating and rival feature models against the same response under direct-linear and geometry-based mappings. Under direct-linear generation, regression outperformed correlation-distance RSA/Spearman but not RSA/Pearson. Under geometry-linear generation, neither approach showed a significant advantage, whereas correlation-distance RSA outperformed regression under a sigmoid readout, although this advantage did not extend to Euclidean-distance RSA. Feature dimensionality, covariance, and PCA preprocessing further influenced model-selection accuracy. In multivoxel simulations, pattern component modeling (PCM) showed superior performance under spatially correlated noise, while crossnobis improved selection relative to squared Euclidean RSA under the same condition. Feature reweighting improved held-out representational correspondence for both generating and rival models without consistently improving model selection. Separate signal-strength comparisons yielded different method rankings. In empirical data from 4,026 words, all methods preferred frequency over affect features, but small-sample preference stability depended on distance measures and preprocessing. Together, these findings demonstrate that model-selection performance depends on stimulus-response mappings, representational geometry, measurement noise, and inferential objectives.
