Who's Afraid of the Wallenius Distribution?


Linda M. Haines
[stat.ME]

This paper is about the use of the Wallenius noncentral hypergeometric distribution for analysing tables generated from a sequential biased sampling scheme without replacement and thus with row margins and sample size fixed. The parameters of the distribution are taken to be weights which are positive and sum to one and are thus defined on a regular simplex. The approach to analysis is presented for likelihood-based and Bayesian inference and is illustrated by example, with datasets taken from the literature or generated from semi-synthetic data. The analysis of the $2 \times 2$ tables using the univariate Wallenius distribution is shown to be straightforward, with the parameter a single weight which translates immediately to the requisite odds and odds ratio. The analysis of tables with more than two categories based on the multivariate Wallenius distribution was however more nuanced than that of the two-category tables. Specifically, some numerical subtleties were required in order to implement the necessary calculations and a Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm for the Bayesian analysis was introduced.
