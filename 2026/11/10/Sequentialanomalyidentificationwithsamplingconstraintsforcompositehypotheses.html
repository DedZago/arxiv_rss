Sequential anomaly identification with sampling constraints for composite hypotheses


Aristomenis Tsopelakos, Georgios Fellouris, Olgica Milenkovic
[stat.ME,stat.TH]

We consider the problem of sequential anomaly identification with sampling constraints, where multiple independent data sources are observed over time, but only a limited subset can be sampled at each instant. The objective is to identify the anomalous sources while controlling the probabilities of at least one false alarm and at least one missed detection below prescribed levels. An identification policy must jointly determine when to stop sampling, which sources to declare anomalous, and how to allocate observations across sources under the sampling constraints. We seek policies that minimize the expected time to decision to a first-order asymptotic approximation as the error probabilities vanish. The observations from each source follow an unknown density belonging to one of two disjoint sets, corresponding to regular and anomalous behavior. Focusing on a nonparametric framework, which allows greater flexibility in modeling regular and anomalous statistical behavior, we establish general conditions for asymptotic optimality and provide a unified characterization of asymptotically optimal policies for composite hypothesis testing under sampling constraints. Simulation results illustrate the performance of the proposed policies, and the robustness of the nonparametric framework.
