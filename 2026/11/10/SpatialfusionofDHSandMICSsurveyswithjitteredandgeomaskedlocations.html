Spatial fusion of DHS and MICS surveys with jittered and geomasked locations


John Paige, Geir-Arne Fuglstad, Andrea Riebler
[stat.ME]

In low- and middle-income countries, survey data is a key data source for public health monitoring. Not accounting for the anonymization applied to the GPS locations in these surveys may lead to bias, and in turn sub-optimal public health decision-making and outcomes. We propose a spatial fusion model to account both for jittering (a form of random perturbation of GPS coordinates) and geomasking (reporting only the name of the area containing the true GPS coordinates). This allows us to combine data from surveys with different kinds of positional uncertainty, reduce bias, and give more precise estimates. We use this approach to estimate women’s secondary education completion through a fusion model using both the 2018 Nigeria demographic and health survey (DHS), with jittered point locations, and the 2016 Nigeria multiple indicator cluster (MICS) survey, with geomasked locations. We show that accounting for the positional uncertainty in the surveys and accounting for both DHS and MICS surveys simultaneously can reduce bias while improving precision in parameter estimates and predictions via a simulation study and validation at cluster and areal levels. We recommend the proposed approach particularly in cases where the modeler wishes to model DHS and MICS surveys jointly, where continuously varying spatial covariates are used, and where local targeted spatial estimates are desired.
