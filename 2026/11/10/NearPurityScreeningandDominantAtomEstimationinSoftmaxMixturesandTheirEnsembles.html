Near-Purity Screening and Dominant-Atom Estimation in Softmax Mixtures and Their Ensembles


Xin Bing, Florentina Bunea, Marten Wegkamp, Weicheng Wu
[stat.ME,stat.TH]

We study near-purity screening and dominant-atom estimation in softmax mixtures, whose components share a common set of category features. A mixture is called $\varepsilon$-pure if one of its mixing weights is at least $1-\varepsilon$. We introduce the Trace of Latent Covariance Criterion ($\mathrm{TLC}^{2}$), a direct estimator of the trace of the covariance matrix of the latent mixing measure, constructed without estimating its atoms or weights, or knowing the number of components. Unlike existing moment-based approaches, our estimator is constructed for the softmax family and does not require knowledge or estimation of the feature distribution. We establish finite-sample guarantees for $\mathrm{TLC}^{2}$, showing that it achieves a parametric convergence rate up to an additive approximation error of order $p^{-1/2}$, without requiring minimum separation between mixture atoms. We also study estimation of the dominant atom in near-pure mixtures by fitting a single softmax component. We prove that the resulting maximum likelihood estimator has an error bound consisting of the usual parametric term and a bias term governed by the weighted distances between the dominant and remaining atoms, revealing adaptation to the geometry of the mixture. Finally, we extend near-purity screening and dominant-atom estimation to ensembles of softmax mixtures with shared atoms and mixture-specific weights. We apply $\mathrm{TLC}^{2}$ separately to each ensemble member to screen for near-purity without knowing the number of components. Under additional assumptions, clustering the resulting dominant-atom estimators yields finite-sample guarantees for estimating the shared atoms. An application to the Million Song Dataset illustrates how the estimated softmax directions reveal interpretable structure in the data.
