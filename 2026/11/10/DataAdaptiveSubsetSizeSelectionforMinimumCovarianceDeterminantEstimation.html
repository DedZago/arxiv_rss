Data-Adaptive Subset-Size Selection for Minimum Covariance Determinant Estimation


Chikun Li, Baisuo Jin
[stat.ME]

Subset size determines the balance between robustness and efficiency in minimum covariance determinant estimation. Conventional choices fix this balance before the extent and structure of contamination are known. Reweighting does not remove the influence of the initial subset size. We propose a data-adaptive rule based on ordered squared Mahalanobis distances. It selects the first window maximum, with window width proportional to the sample size. For a minimum covariance determinant subset, the statistic equals the smallest squared distance among excluded observations. For smooth elliptical distributions with small one-point contamination, we characterize the first population window maximum. Under suitable conditions, it is the last retained fraction at which excluding the contaminating point remains optimal. We derive a first-order expansion for this boundary and establish sample localization along the full integer path. Without contamination, the selected fraction tends to one. A fast depth-based implementation reuses one ordering across candidate sizes. Simulations under Gaussian and heavy-tailed sampling show gains over fixed subset sizes, most consistently for scatter estimation. Real data analyses show that the initial subset size affects which observations are retained after reweighting.
