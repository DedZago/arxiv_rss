Short-term associations between wild-bird and mammalian high pathogenic avian influenza detection in the United States


Hammed Olawale Fatoyinbo
[stat.AP,stat.CO]

High pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) detections in mammals have raised concern about repeated wildlife spillover and the targeting of mammalian surveillance. We evaluated whether wild-bird detection records contained short-term explanatory and predictive information about mammalian detections in the United States. Public surveillance records from April 2022 to July 2026 comprised 18,523 wild-bird and 815 mammalian detections. Monthly state-level mammalian counts were analysed using negative-binomial mixed models adjusted for seasonality, preceding mammalian detections and state-level heterogeneity. The selected model contained contemporaneous and one-month-lagged same-state wild-bird counts. A doubling of one plus the contemporaneous wild-bird count was associated with a 57.9% increase in the expected mammalian count (95% CI 43.5-73.8%), and a doubling of the preceding-month count with a 15.9% increase (5.2-27.8%). Bordering-state exposure did not improve fit. In a six-month temporal holdout, adding same-state wild-bird exposure reduced mean absolute error by 23.8% and root mean squared error by 27.9%, although total detections remained overpredicted. After exact-row deduplication, the corresponding effects were 59.7% and 13.3%. Wild-bird surveillance records contained short-term, geographically local information about mammalian HPAI detection activity, but the associations do not establish direct transmission and require further calibration before operational forecasting.
