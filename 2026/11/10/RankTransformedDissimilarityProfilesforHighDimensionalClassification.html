Rank-Transformed Dissimilarity Profiles for High-Dimensional Classification


Xiangbo Mo, Hao Chen
[stat.ME]

Despite advances in representation learning, high-dimensional classification remains challenging in low-sample-size regimes, where the dominant signal may vary across applications and labeled data are often limited. We propose a dissimilarity-profiling classification framework that represents each observation by its class-wise dissimilarity profile, transforming the original feature space into a low-dimensional representation that summarizes how the observation relates to each class. The key idea is to turn an effect of the curse of dimensionality into signal: high-dimensional geometry can induce systematic within-class and between-class dissimilarity patterns under location, scale, or other distributional changes, and these patterns are captured by the class-wise profiles. Building on this representation, we introduce a rank-transformed algorithm that converts dissimilarities into class-wise rank profiles. The proposed method delivers competitive or improved performance relative to commonly used classifiers on two-class, multi-class, network, and real high-dimensional low-sample-size datasets. To clarify the underlying mechanism, we characterize the mean and covariance of a distance-based surrogate, showing how location, scale, and higher-order distributional features enter the profile representation. Contamination experiments show that the rank transformation can improve robustness. The same profile construction applies to non-Euclidean data whenever pairwise dissimilarities can be computed.
