An Exact Poisson Mixture Representation of Siler Remaining Life Expectancy


Silvio C. Patricio
[stat.AP,stat.ME]

The Siler model describes mortality from birth to old age as the sum of a declining immaturity hazard, a constant background hazard, and an increasing senescence hazard. Its remaining life expectancy has no known closed form, and numerical evaluation alone does not reveal how the immaturity hazard enters it. We show that the remaining-lifetime distribution at age $x$ is Gompertz–Makeham with a Poisson-distributed Makeham term. Successive Makeham terms differ by the rate at which immaturity mortality declines, and the Poisson mean is the cumulative immaturity hazard remaining after age $x$. In a latent-clock construction, individuals carry a Poisson number of constant-rate clocks, and selection removes those carrying more clocks first. Integrating the mixture yields an exact series for remaining life expectancy with a closed-form truncation bound. The life expectancy lost to immaturity mortality is close to the probability of carrying a clock times life expectancy with that hazard removed, with a closed-form error bound. In annual fits to Swedish female mortality, this loss at birth fell from 4.3 years in 1900 to less than 0.01 years in 2020, a decline equal to 15\% of the gain in life expectancy at birth, and the approximation was always within 0.06 years.
