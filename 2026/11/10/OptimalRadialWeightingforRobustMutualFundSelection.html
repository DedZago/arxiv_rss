Optimal Radial Weighting for Robust Mutual Fund Selection


Ping Zhao, Qianya Sun, Long Feng
[stat.ME]

Identifying skilled mutual funds requires methods that distinguish positive alphas from sampling variation when many funds are evaluated simultaneously. Heavy-tailed returns and dependence across funds complicate this task. We propose a robust selection procedure based on radially weighted alpha estimation in linear factor pricing models. The method uses both the directions and lengths of standardized residuals, estimates marginal scales from pairwise residual differences, and accounts for the estimation of factor loadings. Within the proposed class, inverse-norm weighting minimizes the asymptotic variance of the alpha estimators and maximizes the local asymptotic power of individual tests at a common significance level. For radial weights fixed conditional on the factor design, we establish asymptotic false discovery rate control under elliptically symmetric errors and show that the proportion of sufficiently strong positive-alpha funds selected tends to one. The theory accommodates comparable cross-sectional and time-series dimensions and nonvanishing alphas. We also develop a smoothed implementation and assess its finite-sample properties. Simulations show higher true discovery proportions than least-squares and spatial-sign methods under heavy-tailed distributions and improved numerical stability from smoothing. An application to U.S. equity mutual funds examines the subsequent performance of portfolios formed from the selected funds.
