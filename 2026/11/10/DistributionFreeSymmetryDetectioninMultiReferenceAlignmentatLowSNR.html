Distribution-Free Symmetry Detection in Multi-Reference Alignment at Low SNR


Nir Sharon, Sheng Xu
[stat.ME,stat.TH]

Symmetry can reveal the structure of a signal, but detecting it becomes difficult when observations are noisy and randomly transformed. We study how to infer symmetry directly from multi-reference alignment data with additive isotropic Gaussian noise of known variance, without aligning the observations or knowing how the transformations were sampled. For cyclic symmetry, we identify a simple statistical signature that yields tests with exact finite-sample error guarantees. We demonstrate their performance even at very low signal-to-noise ratios. Dihedral symmetry presents a deeper challenge: we show that, for some signals, invariant moments below a sharp order threshold cannot distinguish reflectional from purely rotational symmetry. For generic signals, however, this distinction emerges much earlier. We use these insights to develop tests for dihedral symmetry with asymptotic error control under explicit nondegeneracy conditions. Together, the results clarify what we can learn about symmetry from noisy, unaligned data and suggest a path toward symmetry inference in cryo-EM.
