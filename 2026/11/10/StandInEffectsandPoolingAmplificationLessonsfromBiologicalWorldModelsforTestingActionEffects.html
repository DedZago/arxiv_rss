Stand-In Effects and Pooling Amplification: Lessons from Biological World Models for Testing Action Effects


Bowen Xu, Boyu Chen, Jiaying Li
[stat.ME]

Whether an action-conditioned world model uses actions is usually checked by replacing or removing them and measuring lost accuracy. With biological experiments as the guiding case, we show that such diagnostics report action effects where there are none: under autocorrelated designs, future actions stand in for history the model does not see, and a better model learns the stand-in better. When the design law is known, a conditional randomization test written as an e-value is valid in finite samples whatever the model, provided the resampler conditions on the full state the design used; resampling from a simpler law reproduces the false effect. A second failure is specific to world models: when many sites share one action sequence (cells in a microscope field, pixels in a frame), the pooled score turns small model errors along the action contrast into overwhelming evidence against a true effect. In a pixel world, its median log e-value fell from $-17.3$ to $-6832$ as frames grew from 64 to 4096 pixels, while the oracle’s rose. Our preregistered analysis of optogenetic ERK experiments failed this way; a tempered statistic, selected on synthetic data and registered before the data were obtained, then rejected the null on a never-opened field ($E\approx1.1\times10^{10}$). Tempering factors estimated from residuals can err by an order of magnitude, whereas a mixture over a fixed grid of factors needs no estimate and loses at most the logarithm of the grid size in log e-value relative to its best member. In a registered test on a diffusion world model for three Atari games released by others, the pooled test rejected in 0 of 96 units in which actions mattered and Kish and contrast tempering in 0 and 1, while the mixture rejected in 96; under an exact null, eight statistics made 0 rejections in 1,536 tests. Every analysis is preregistered, fixed in a dated protocol, or labelled exploratory.
