Graph Fourier Neural Kernels: Learning Full PDE Trajectories Across Geometries and Anisotropic Parameter Fields


Shane E. Loeffler, Zan Ahmad, Shiyi Chen, Syed Yusuf Ali, Carolyna Yamamoto, Kensuke Sakata, Dan M. Popescu, Alana Yee, Yash Lal, Natalia Trayanova, Mauro Maggioni
[stat.ME,stat.ML]

High-fidelity simulations of time-dependent nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs) are expensive to repeat across families of domains and parameter fields, a cost that recurs in design optimization, uncertainty quantification, and computational medicine. Most neural-operator surrogates require each domain to be mapped onto a common grid or reference shape, and few predict the full time course. We introduce Graph Fourier Neural Kernels (G-FuNK), a neural operator for second-order semilinear PDEs with a diffusive principal part that transfers across geometries and anisotropic diffusion tensors without coordinate maps or diffeomorphisms. A weighted graph and its Laplacian supply a spectral basis that encodes geometry and anisotropy. Shared layers act in this basis, at a cost linear in mesh size after one partial eigendecomposition per geometry, and a neural ODE integrator propagates the learned generator to full spatiotemporal trajectories on unseen domains. Across anisotropic heat flow, stiff reaction-diffusion, FitzHugh-Nagumo, and patient-specific left-atrial electrophysiology on CT-derived surfaces, G-FuNK reproduces held-out trajectories with relative $L^2$ error between 0.046 and 0.160. Its accuracy is comparable to that of a geometry-aware Fourier neural operator on regular domains, and its predictions are unchanged when a rectangular domain is rotated. On left atria, whose venous and valvular openings prevent mapping onto a reference shape, it predicts an 85-ms trajectory in under half a second, against about 5 minutes for finite elements, with a mean activation-time error of 1.73 ms. Accuracy depends on graph construction and is highest when the learned generator is integrated at its training time step.
