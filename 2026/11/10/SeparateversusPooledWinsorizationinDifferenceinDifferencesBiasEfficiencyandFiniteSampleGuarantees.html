Separate versus Pooled Winsorization in Difference-in-Differences: Bias, Efficiency, and Finite-Sample Guarantees


Chao Cheng , Chenshan Hu , Yukai Huang
[stat.ME,stat.AP]

\textit{\textbf{Problem Definition: }} Extreme outcomes are common in operational data, and empirical difference-in-differences (DiD) studies often winsorize them to limit their influence. Yet winsorization can change both the estimated treatment effect and its sampling uncertainty. We ask when winsorization improves DiD estimation and whether cutoffs should be computed separately within each group-period cell or from observations pooled across cells. \textit{\textbf{Methodology/Results: }} We develop finite-sample and asymptotic analyses in a two-group, two-period DiD setting. For continuous outcomes with finite variance, separate winsorization achieves a uniform sub-Gaussian deviation guarantee under a calibrated winsorization fraction, while neither raw DiD nor pooled winsorization can achieve the same guarantee. We identify conditions under which separate winsorization is asymptotically unbiased, whereas pooled winsorization with a fixed fraction may remain biased. Under these conditions, standard tail classifications alone do not establish an efficiency ranking. Yet a two-sided decreasing-failure-rate (DFR) condition on each cell’s error distribution can guarantee strictly smaller asymptotic variance for separate winsorization than for raw DiD at every fixed winsorization fraction. Numerical results support both analyses. \textit{\textbf{Managerial Implications: }} Our results guide practitioners on whether and how to winsorize outcomes in DiD studies, clarifying the advantages and limitations of raw DiD, separate winsorization, and pooled winsorization. The DFR condition arising from our efficiency analysis offers a simple diagnostic for when winsorization can be expected to lower asymptotic variance while keeping the treatment-effect estimate unbiased, giving theoretical grounding to a practice that has so far rested mainly on convention.
