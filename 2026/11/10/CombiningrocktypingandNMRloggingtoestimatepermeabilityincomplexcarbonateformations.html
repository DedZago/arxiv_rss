Combining rock typing and NMR logging to estimate permeability in complex carbonate formations


Nihal Malavalli Lokesh, Biswajit Thander, Esmail Ansari, Ali Eghbali, Behzad Ghanbarian
[stat.AP]

Estimating intrinsic permeability from nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) logs is difficult in heterogeneous carbonates because a single calibration may not represent distinct pore systems. We evaluated whether flow-zone-indicator (FZI)-based rock typing improves empirical calibration of the modified Schlumberger-Doll Research (SDR) model in the Arbuckle Group, Kansas. The analysis used 470 depth-matched core-permeability and NMR measurements from the Wellington and Cutter wells. FZI calculated from measured permeability and NMR-derived porosity was grouped using K-means clustering. Six clusters were identified, but one singleton cluster was excluded, leaving five rock types for SDR calibration. Relative to the global model (without clustering), rock-type-specific calibration reduced testing root mean square log-transformed error (RMSLE) from 1.215 to 0.240-0.427 and increased coefficient of determination (CD) from -0.106 to 0.578-0.924. The fitted porosity exponents ranged from 0.053 to 0.521, indicating that porosity provided limited additional information in some rock types and that the SDR parameters should be treated as empirical calibration quantities. Because measured permeability was used to calculate the FZI before the within-cluster splits, these results represent conditional performance for samples with known FZI membership, not blind permeability prediction. Field implementation will require a permeability-independent method for assigning rock types.
