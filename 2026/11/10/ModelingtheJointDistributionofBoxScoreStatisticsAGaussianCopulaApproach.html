Modeling the Joint Distribution of Box Score Statistics: A Gaussian Copula Approach


Kenny Watts
[stat.AP]

What is the probability that LeBron James records at least 27 points, 7 rebounds, and 7 assists tonight? While an interesting statistical question by itself, the rise of sports betting and multi-leg parlays has made answering this question increasingly relevant. However, coming up with an estimated likelihood is tricky, since box score statistics aren’t independent. We estimate the joint probability that a player reaches a combination of thresholds in points, assists, and rebounds using a Gaussian copula, fit separately for each player using only their previous games. We compare it with two benchmarks: the product of the empirical marginal probabilities (i.e. assuming independence) and the empirical frequency of the joint event. Across ~22,000 player-observations, the Gaussian copula performs on par or better than either benchmark at lower threshold levels, with large gains at high joint exceedance levels. Because it is generative, the model also allows for probabilistic calculations for combinations that a player has rarely or never produced.
