A Review of Measurement System Uncertainty Handoffs Across the Manufacturing Decision Lifecycle


Fei Jiang, Lei Yang
[stat.AP]

Measurement systems generate uncertain information that downstream manufacturing decisions often consume as if fixed. This structured integrative review examines how measurement error, uncertainty, GR\&R estimates, calibration evidence, and drift information are handed off into capability inference, retrospective Phase I reference estimation, prospective Phase II monitoring, operational action, and requalification. Studies are compared by the upstream uncertain object, whether it is known, calibrated, or estimated, its propagation mechanism, the downstream decision, and the validation boundary. The synthesis distinguishes recorded production data from latent-process targets: measurement-system information calibrates limits, uncertainty statements, replication, guard bands, or losses, but does not reveal the realized random error in an individual record. Within the retained literature, measurement-to-capability and measurement-to-monitoring mechanisms are more extensively represented by model-based studies, whereas prospective evidence is thinner where statistical results must support source attribution, cost-sensitive action, and requalification. A decision-handoff spine separates measurement generation, statistical inference, and operational validation so that capability, stability, attribution, and intervention effectiveness are not conflated. The review is integrative rather than exhaustive. Its contribution is an auditable measurement-science framework that preserves provenance, information status, validity conditions, and downstream use while identifying claims that are model-supported, conditionally calibrated, or not yet operationally validated.
