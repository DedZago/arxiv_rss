An Exact Marginal Likelihood for Inference under Careless Responding


Jaehoan Kim
[stat.ME,stat.CO,stat.TH]

Careless or insufficient-effort responding threatens the validity of self-report measurement. Mixture models for careless responding specify its form, and screening rules and robust methods depend on a threshold or tuning constant with no direct meaning in terms of careless responses. We propose ExHuber, a Bayesian method for inference on the engaged population under Huber’s contamination model, in which an unknown proportion of responses comes from a contaminating distribution of unspecified form instead of the structural model. With a finite support of responses, such as the values of a scale score, a Dirichlet prior and a Beta prior on the contaminating distribution and its proportion yield a closed-form exact marginal likelihood of the structural parameters. Under a prior favoring a contaminating distribution on a few values, we show that the posterior of the structural parameters is consistent when careless responses occupy few values of the support, without knowing which values. Dynamic programming evaluates the marginal likelihood and its gradient at a cost quadratic in the sample size, which enables gradient-based sampling. Simulations from graded response models show accurate inference under straightlining and repeated response patterns, and an application to Big Five scales estimates the engaged mean of each scale.
