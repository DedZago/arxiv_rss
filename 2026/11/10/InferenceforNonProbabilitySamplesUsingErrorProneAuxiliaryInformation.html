Inference for Non-Probability Samples Using Error-Prone Auxiliary Information


Lyndon Ang, Robert Clark, Bronwyn Loong, Anders Holmberg
[stat.ME]

Non-probability samples are a potential source of rich information for making statistical inferences. However, these datasets often suffer from quality issues, in particular selection bias. The data from a non-probability sample may be combined with auxiliary information from the population or from a reference probability sample to address this bias. The auxiliary information present on these other sources is generally considered to be error-free, however this is not always the case. In this paper, we present a novel semiparametric approach to estimate the participation probabilities of units on a non-probability sample while accounting for error-prone auxiliary information in the reference data source. The approach combines a logistic regression model for the participation probabilities with a nonparametric kernel regression model for the error-prone auxiliary variables. Through a simulation study and real-world data analysis we show that our proposed approach effectively mitigates selection bias and accounts for measurement error, while estimates from methods which ignore the measurement error still exhibit some bias. The consistency of the Inverse Probability Weighted estimator produced using the participation probabilities derived from our approach is demonstrated, and a bootstrap variance approach for the estimator is described.
