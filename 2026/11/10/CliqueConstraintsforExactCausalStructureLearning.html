Clique Constraints for Exact Causal Structure Learning


Xiaoyu He, Pavel Rytíř, Petr Ryšavý, Jakub Mareček
[stat.ML]

Exact score-based causal structure learning supports explicit structural constraints and optimisation certificates, but its branch-and-cut search can be computationally demanding. We augment exact causal structure learning methods with an upper bound on the maximum clique size of the learned undirected skeleton, enforced at integer candidates through lazy cycle and clique constraints. On static linear-Gaussian Erd\H{o}s-R'enyi and scale-free DAGs, we evaluate callback order, alternative clique-cut policies, incumbent repair, fractional separation, and Big-M bounds. We report causal-recovery metrics jointly with optimality gaps, primal and dual bounds, node throughput, MIPSOL events, and callback cost. Two variants receive particular attention: B3 selects the most violated minimal (k+1)-clique inequality at each callback, whereas C3 repairs an infeasible integer candidate by deleting violating edges and refitting affected regression columns using QR-based least squares. The results do not indicate that clique constraints uniformly accelerate optimisation. However, their benefit depends on graph density, sample size, and cut-management policy, while large collections of correlated constraints can reduce node throughput. Tighter valid Big-M bounds reduced the fixed-budget optimality gap in most tested settings, without necessarily changing SHD or F1 at a fixed time limit. These findings characterize the computational trade-offs of clique-constrained exact causal learning, and identify selective separation and incumbent repair as promising directions.
