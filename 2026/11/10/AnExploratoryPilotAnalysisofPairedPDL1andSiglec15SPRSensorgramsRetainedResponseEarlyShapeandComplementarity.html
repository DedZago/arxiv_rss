An Exploratory Pilot Analysis of Paired PD-L1 and Siglec-15 SPR Sensorgrams: Retained Response, Early Shape, and Complementarity


Pengfei Wang
[stat.AP]

We conduct a retrospective exploratory pilot analysis of paired PD-L1 and Siglec-15 surface plasmon resonance (SPR) trajectories, examining retained response, dual-marker fusion, and additional early-shape information. Numerical matching links the original exports to previously quality-selected data: 69 files represent 68 distinct source column pairs from 10 cancer and 10 healthy study patients. Existing exclusions and truncations are preserved. All 20 patients contribute to development and exploration; no independent validation cohort is available. Because historical per-scan manual alignment offsets are unavailable, the numerical comparisons are conditional on separately documented candidate response-transition intervals rather than verified injection-event times. Patient-level paired comparisons, leave-pair-out internal assessment, empirical false-positive budgets, and timing sensitivity are implemented in reproducible Python code. Under candidate alignment, the dual-marker retained-response sum has an apparent AUC of 0.570, with a fixed-score bootstrap 95% interval of [0.300, 0.820]. In the nested 13-patient shape subset, retention alone and the fixed 85:15 retention-shape score both have AUC 0.619; the small paired increment changes direction across the -1,0,+1 minute shift scenarios. At a same-sample budget of two false positives among ten controls, dual-marker retention identifies four cancer cases, compared with two for PD-L1 and three for Siglec-15. Analytical bounds describe feature stability under stated measurement assumptions but do not identify the cause of weak discrimination. The study provides a traceable analysis framework and operating-point hypotheses for independent evaluation; the small development cohort does not establish diagnostic utility or a reliable fusion or shape advantage.
