Toward a Unified Statistical Theory of Multi-View Pretraining for Neural Knowledge Graph Learning


Jifan Zhang, Miklos Racz, Suqi Liu
[stat.ME,stat.ML,stat.TH]

Knowledge graph learning offers a powerful framework for representing and inferring structured knowledge, with broad practical applications. Two obstacles limit current methods: labeled triples are scarce for each entity and relation, which makes expressive models hard to train, and scoring functions are designed ad hoc, which limits their generalizability and leaves them without theoretical grounding. We address both obstacles with a theoretically grounded framework, with end-to-end guarantees, that extends and subsumes existing methods. The framework proceeds in two stages: multi-view unsupervised pretraining that integrates heterogeneous sources of entity side information into a shared representation, followed by supervised learning across multiple relation types. We establish a unified nonasymptotic risk bound that combines the subspace recovery error of pretraining and the sample complexity of the labeled data in a single guarantee, quantifying formally how large-scale unlabeled data from multiple views benefits downstream knowledge prediction. Synthetic experiments validate each component of the theory, and experiments on large-scale real-world knowledge graph benchmarks confirm the effectiveness of our approach.
