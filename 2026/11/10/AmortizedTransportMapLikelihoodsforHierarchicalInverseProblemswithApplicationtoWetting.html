Amortized Transport-Map Likelihoods for Hierarchical Inverse Problems with Application to Wetting


Henning Bonart
[stat.AP]

Hierarchical Bayesian models pool measurements, but with the forward model in the likelihood every posterior evaluation costs one forward solve per cell. To reduce this cost, we introduce a simulation-based inference framework in which a triangular transport-map likelihood, trained once per design, serves every cell. We apply it to infer wetting properties from photographs of a stable hole in a thin liquid film. The hole’s radius depends, through the nonlinear Young-Laplace equation, on the contact angle and capillary length. One photograph gives rank-one information about them, so we pool many in a hierarchical model whose cells, each a substrate at one volume setting, share the apparatus geometry and the noise scale. A design fixes the number of substrates, the volume settings and the photographs per cell. The map’s posteriors agree with direct sampling of the forward model in the wetting properties at a synthetic validation design, and less closely at a larger synthetic scaling design. At the validation design, simulation-based calibration detects no departure for any parameter. There, the map needs several hundred times fewer forward-model evaluations than direct sampling with a gradient-free ensemble sampler for a single posterior, and about five orders of magnitude fewer over a calibration study of 200 posteriors. On real photographs its posterior agrees with direct sampling and is consistent with the measured radii. Trained offline, the amortized map turns new photographs into a posterior with no forward solve. It is thus a major step toward measuring wetting properties in situ without disturbing the liquid.
