Modeling inter-exceedance times of extreme heat days


Merle Mendel, Roland Fried, Nils Weitzel
[stat.AP]

Extreme heat days, here defined as exceedances of a local calendar-day-based 95% quantile, often occur in clusters forming multi-day heatwaves. A calendar-day based threshold allows for the detection of temperature extremes across all seasons, which may impact several economic sectors, public health and ecosystems. Previous approaches to modeling the occurrences of extreme heat events typically treat heat event duration and the time between heat events separately. We propose modeling the inter-exceedance times (IETs) between extreme heat days, allowing for a unified description within a single model. We consider several candidate distributions for the IETs of extreme heat days and find that the zero-inflated discrete Weibull (ZIDW) distribution clearly provides the best fit according to Pearson’s chi-squared goodness-of-fit tests. The ZIDW distribution is a flexible model for count data exhibiting excess zeros as well as over- and under-dispersion: the zero-inflation component captures the pronounced clustering of heat days, while the discrete Weibull component adequately accounts for over-dispersion. Heat days are extracted using both trend-adjusted and non-trend-adjusted thresholds. This allows the analysis of general changes in the occurrence of extreme heat days as well as changes after accounting for the general increase in temperature.
