A uniform improvement to conformal risk control via a generalization of e-closure


Tian Bai, Aaditya Ramdas
[stat.ME,stat.ML,stat.TH]

We extend the recently introduced e-closure principle for multiple testing to general state and action spaces and to arbitrary, including continuously indexed, families of risks. A collection of e-values indexed by states and risks certifies a menu of actions, and every data-dependent selection from this menu controls each expected loss at its prescribed level. Conversely, every valid procedure is certified by its own normalized losses, which often leave part of the risk budget unused. We introduce a method called recertification, which spends this unused budget. Using known distributional structure, it enlarges the certifying e-values while preserving validity and selects a less conservative action from the enlarged menu. One particularly compelling application is to the popular framework of conformal risk control (CRC) with a known loss family. Here, recertification redistributes unused risk budget among the held-out points of each hypothetical augmented sample. The resulting controller is valid under every finite exchangeable law and less (usually strictly) conservative than CRC. For convex losses on an interval of actions, repeated recertification converges geometrically to an admissible controller. As a second example involving false discovery rate control with e-values, recertifying randomized e-BH yields a closed-form procedure that rejects a superset of randomized e-BH for every realization of the randomization and maximizes the expected number of rejections under the same constraints. Recertifying with the larger e-collection of closed e-BH gives a second closed-form procedure that rejects a superset of the first. Experiments on CIFAR-100 hierarchical classification and simulations demonstrate improved budget utilization and gains in informativeness and power over the corresponding baselines.
