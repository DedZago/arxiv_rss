Perceived vertical and eye level as one orientation order parameter: a closed-form account of the Li-Matin rules for egocentric space


A. Y. Shavit
[stat.AP]

Li and Matin (2005a, 2005b) reported three regularities of the induced visually-perceived vertical (VPV): a roll-tilted line shifts the perceived vertical approximately linearly with its orientation; two lines combine approximately linearly; and symmetric tilts cancel. We show all three follow from one principle. The induced vertical is half the argument of the first circular moment of the stimulus orientation distribution in the doubled-angle domain, phi = (1/2) arg(c_1) with c_1 = sum_j A_j exp(i 2 gamma_j) (the orientation order-parameter model, PLUMB). We prove c_1 is the unique additive, salience-homogeneous axial summary of an orientation field: it turns by twice the angle when the field rotates (spin-2, rotation-covariant), while the axis it encodes turns by the angle itself. The angle-doubling is forced by linear pooling plus rotation covariance, not assumed. Linear tracking, weighted combination, and symmetric cancellation follow as signatures of that readout. Li and Matin’s 2-, 3-, and 4-line combination coefficients are matched with no per-configuration free parameters in the angles; one saturating gain function of length sets the overall gain. The model predicts n equal inducers combine sub-additively, approaching averaging slope 1/n as gain saturates. The data refute complete summation but do not separate it from simple averaging for long lines; decisive tests lie in short lines and a coherence law vanishing at 45 degrees. Decomposing the orientation signal per hemifield into sum and difference readouts recovers perceived eye level (VPEL) as the antisymmetric counterpart to VPV, matching Shavit, Li, and Matin’s (2013) reversed integration rules; trial-level data (N=30) confirm the predicted sign and null structure. The account is stimulus-side and image-computable, linking perceived vertical and eye level to classical descriptors of image orientation.
