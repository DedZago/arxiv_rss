Finite-Sample Risk Approximation and Risk-Consistent Tuning for Generalized Ridge Estimators in Nonlinear Models


Masamune Iwasawa
[stat.ME]

This paper studies the finite-sample risk properties of generalized ridge estimators in nonlinear likelihood models. Using a finite-sample approximation approach, we derive an explicit approximation to the mean squared error (MSE) of the coefficient estimator. We show that ridge regularization introduces a first-order bias–variance trade-off and can reduce finite-sample MSE even under target misspecification. We propose a data-driven selection rule based on a Stein-type approximation, where risk is defined as the trace of the MSE, and show that it achieves oracle risk consistency. A key implication is that finite-sample risk is often dominated by rare but extreme realizations, and the proposed method stabilizes such extreme realizations, substantially reducing MSE. Simulation and empirical results illustrate the practical relevance of the approach, clarify the mechanism underlying the MSE improvements, and demonstrate its implications for treatment effect estimation.
