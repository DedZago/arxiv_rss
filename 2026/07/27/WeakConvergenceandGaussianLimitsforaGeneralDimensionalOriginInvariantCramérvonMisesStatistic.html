Weak Convergence and Gaussian Limits for a General-Dimensional Origin-Invariant Cramér--von Mises Statistic


Marco Mandap
[stat.ME,stat.TH]

We introduce a general-dimensional origin-invariant Cram'er–von Mises statistic $\bar{\omega}_d^2$ for testing complete spatial randomness, defined by averaging corner-oriented empirical-distribution-function discrepancies over all $2^d$ corners of $[0,1]^d$. The statistic has a closed-form $O(n^2)$ computing formula. For $d=1$ it reduces to the classical rank-based Cram'er–von Mises statistic, while for $d=2$ its computing formula agrees with Zimmerman’s origin-invariant statistic. Under iid complete spatial randomness, the associated empirical process converges in $\ell^\infty$ to a centered Gaussian process with an explicit cross-corner covariance kernel. The continuous mapping theorem yields a quadratic Gaussian limit governed by a positive trace-class covariance operator with trace $2^{-d}-3^{-d}$. We also connect fixed-count complete spatial randomness with the homogeneous Poisson formulation. For strictly stationary alpha-mixing sequences with uniform marginals, we establish covariance summability, the long-run variance limit, and a finite-dimensional all-corners Gaussian limit. Monte Carlo experiments illustrate null calibration and sensitivity to selected alternatives. The statistic is consistent against fixed iid alternatives whose distribution functions differ from uniformity on a set of positive measure.
