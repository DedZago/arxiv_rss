A New Look at Bayesian Testing


Jyotishka Datta, Nicholas G. Polson, Vadim Sokolov, Daniel Zantedeschi
[stat.ME,stat.TH]

We study the asymptotic calibration of rejection thresholds for composite hypothesis tests under integrated (Bayes) risk. The risk-optimal rejection boundary lies on the moderate deviation scale $n\lambda_n^2 \asymp \log n$, yielding critical values of order $\sqrt{\log n}$ in contrast to the sample-size-invariant calibrations of classical testing. We state explicit assumptions (Cram'er regularity, local prior smoothness, symmetric loss) under which Bayes risk minimization selects the moderate deviation boundary. A uniform moderate deviation lemma provides the tail approximations that drive the analysis. We derive the exact threshold $t_{\mathrm{crit}} = \sqrt{\log(\pi n/2)}$ for the Cauchy prior via Laplace expansion and show that the $\sqrt{\log n}$ scaling is universal across regular priors and extends to one-parameter exponential families under standard local asymptotic normality. The framework unifies Jeffreys’ $\sqrt{\log n}$ threshold, the BIC penalty $(d/2)\log n$, and the Chernoff–Stein error exponents as consequences of moderate deviation analysis of Bayes risk. We extend the Rubin & Sethuraman (1965a) program to contemporary settings including high-dimensional sparse inference and model selection, and provide algebraic comparisons with fixed-$\alpha$ and e-value calibrations.
